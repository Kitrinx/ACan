Sound Blaster .VOC files found inside the cartridges, with .wav conversions.

They are plain unsigned 8-bit PCM at 8 kHz, and that is how the games play
them: Sango Fighter copies the bytes straight into the sound chip's RAM and
plays them raw, which was watched happening in the emulator. Ignore what the
headers say. Two header bytes in every file are wrong - the header-size field
reads 282 instead of 26, and the "pack" byte claims 4-bit ADPCM on 67 of the
76 files - so a real VOC player will misread them. The .wav files beside each
.voc are the bytes written out as PCM, which is correct.

Sango Fighter: 75 voice clips, the fighters' shouts and grunts, copied from
the PC original with the PC container headers still attached.

Gambling Overlord Mahjong: one file, and nothing in the game ever plays it.
Its header claims 8.7 seconds, but only the first 1.75 seconds is audio - a
low swell and a decaying hit, a gong or drum. After that the space the header
claims is occupied by other data from the build, which is why the full-length
.wav turns into a harsh buzz at about two seconds. Both versions are here: the
1.75 second cut is the sound, the full-length one is what the header says.
